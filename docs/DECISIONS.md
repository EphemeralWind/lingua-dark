# Decisions log

Settled in the design discussion:

- **Dialect:** Castilian Spanish from Spain. `vosotros` included, es-ES voice, Spain vocabulary.
- **Saving:** local only for now. Accounts and cross-device sync are deferred.
- **Strike timing:** the strike resolves the moment the player locks in an answer, or when the window closes with no answer.
- **Fleeing:** a started fight cannot be fled.
- **Focus:** removed. Guessing is discouraged by taking the hit, a one-in-four random-guess success rate, stance refill on hits, and the typed riposte.
- **Reading choices:** signposts and notes in exploration count as untelegraphed contexts for mastery.
- **Boss gate:** the locked door in front of a boss's arena. It opens when at least 80% of the zone's components are Learned. *(Superseded 2026-11-17 by #73: the gate needs the zone's seals.)*

### 2026-11-17: Kickoff readings (spec ambiguities)

Each entry: what was ambiguous, the reading picked, and why. Revisit any of them if the design says otherwise.

**Rules and anti-shortcut**

1. **Seven rules vs nine.** CLAUDE.md lists seven non-negotiables; SPEC §4 lists nine anti-shortcut rules. The seven in CLAUDE.md are the binding, tested list. SPEC §4's extra items (feints test reading, guessing is costly, the litmus test) are design intent that the seven already cover. *Why:* CLAUDE.md calls itself the override.
2. **Feint flicker vs "identical wind-up".** A feint flicker does change the wind-up, which looks like it breaks rule 1. Reading: rule 1 means the wind-up must not depend on the question or the answer. Feint flicker is drawn from an animation RNG stream seeded separately from the question/shuffle stream. Its direction and timing are uniform and never read the answer. The blind bot sees the flicker and must still score at chance. *Why:* this keeps feints in the game without leaking anything.
3. **Direction shuffle.** Each strike's correct direction is drawn uniformly and independently. There is no "don't repeat last direction" rule, because that would let a bot score 1/3 by avoiding the last strike direction. The strike animation reveals the correct direction after lock-in, so this independence matters.
4. **Text-shape leak (beyond the spec's blind bot).** A language-blind player can still read card *shapes*. For example, with `levanta / levantas / levantan / levanto`, the answer is the prefix of two other cards. Reading: distractor sets are drawn so that, given the four shown forms, each one is equally likely to be the answer. In practice: pick the answer's feature value uniformly (person first, then a pronoun for that person, so 3sg is not twice as likely just because él and ella both exist), then take a uniform random subset of the paradigm that contains the answer. M1 adds a second bot to the blind-bot suite that uses only card text features (length, shared prefixes, edit-distance medoid) and must also score at chance. *Why:* rule zero is "not winnable without Spanish". The animation-only bot is necessary but not sufficient.
5. **Strike index.** `EncounterState.strike` is the index of the *blank* in a combo, not of an answer. That is allowed. Answers are only ever referred to by `Direction`.
6. **Render reads a view, not state.** The renderer and the blind bot both consume the same `EncounterView` projection. During wind-up, that type has no `correct` field at all. *Why:* the rule is then enforced by the type system, not by discipline.

**Combat**

7. **Combo windows.** "2.5 s per blank" means each blank gets its own 2.5 s window (× zone factor), as SPEC §3.4 says. It is not a shared total.
8. **Combo clear bonus.** `COMBO_CLEAR_BONUS` = 0.25 × `stanceMax`, not 25% of current stance. It applies only if every strike in the combo was parried.
9. **Stance break mid-combo.** Stance clamps at 0 and the break happens right away. Remaining strikes of that combo are skipped. The riposte uses the full sentence with all its blanks.
10. **Riposte.** It uses the sentence of the question that broke stance, with every blank of that question as a typed field. It is untimed in M1 and submitted with Enter. Each correct word deals `RIPOSTE_DAMAGE_PER_WORD`. A wrong word deals nothing and shows the correction. Afterwards stance resets to `stanceMax` and a new exchange starts. Enemy HP only drops during ripostes.
    - *Known risk, kept as spec:* the riposte word was on the winning card moments earlier, so it can be copied. A blind player rarely gets that far, because expected stance change per guessed exchange is 0.25·25 − 0.75·20 ≈ −8.75. Revisit at playtest.
11. **Riposte matching.** Matching ignores case and surrounding whitespace. In Zone 1 it accepts a missing acute accent or diaeresis (á é í ó ú ü) but shows the correction. `ñ` is a separate letter, so `n` for `ñ` is wrong.
12. **Quality on a hit or timeout.** `quality` is still computed (0 on timeout) and stored in `resolving`, but only parries use it.
13. **Hits.** A hit deals the attack's damage tier and refills stance by `HIT_STANCE_REFILL`, capped at `stanceMax`.
14. **Grab "unblockable except by answering".** No special rule. Every attack is blocked only by answering. The phrase is flavour that stresses no dodge exists. Flasks still apply.
15. **Inputs outside wind-up** (during resolving, riposte, or before the first wind-up) are ignored as directions. During the riposte, keys go to the hidden `<input>`, so WASD typing does not fire directions.
16. **Lock-in timestamp.** Input events carry `performance.now()` taken in `platform` when the event fires, and q uses that time. q is not quantised to the 60 Hz step.
17. **Type extensions.** SPEC §9 types are kept as a subset. `windup` gains `startMs` (the wind-up is time-stretched, so it needs progress). `resolving` gains `question` and `untilMs`. An `Encounter` wrapper holds `enemy`, player `vitality`, `attack`, `state`, and the RNG streams.
18. **New tunables.** These are not in the design doc and go into `tuning.ts` marked "default (not in design doc)": `RESOLVE_MS` = 600 (strike/parry animation before the next wind-up), `RIPOSTE_RESULT_MS` = 800, and the damage tier per attack type (jab low, overhead high, delayed medium, grab high, sweep medium, combo medium per strike, per SPEC §3.3).
19. **Attack ids.** `AttackType = 'jab' | 'overhead' | 'delayed' | 'grab' | 'sweep' | 'combo'`, matching `zone1-rooms.json`. These map to `Question.kind` as vocab, grammar, translate, listen, order, combo.

**Content**

20. **Article slots with no `agreesWith`.** `t_definite_article` and `t_indefinite_article` leave out `agreesWith`. Reading: an article slot always agrees with the noun that follows it, and a missing `agreesWith` defaults to the next noun slot. *Why:* the `tests` field (`art:definite:{gender}:sg`) already assumes agreement.
21. **"Change exactly one feature" vs three distractors.** An article blank that varies gender and number has only two one-feature neighbours (`la` → `el`, `las`). Reading: distractors come from the paradigm cells spanned by the template's `vary` list (here `el`, `las`, `los`). One-feature errors are preferred when there are more cells than needed. This also gives the symmetric sets that decision 4 wants.
22. **Pattern spacing.** `{art:definite}{noun}` has no space in the pattern. The generator joins adjacent slot tokens with one space. The tight spacing is kept so contractions (`a el` → `al`, `de el` → `del`) can be added later.
23. **Component id format.** CLAUDE.md uses `v_levantar:present:3sg` and `art:definite:f`, while templates use `verb:{verb}:present:{person}` with person keys such as `el`. Canonical form: `v_levantar:present:3sg` (persons map yo→1sg, tu→2sg, el→3sg, nosotros→1pl, vosotros→2pl, ellos→3pl), `art:definite:f:sg` (number kept, since the plural template tests it separately), and `n_manzana:plural`. M2 makes a targeted edit to the `tests` strings in templates.json to match. This is an edit, not a rewrite.
24. **Template placeholders.** `{person}` / `{person1}` means the person of `subj` / `subj1`, and `{gender}` means the gender of the noun the blank agrees with.
25. **No English glosses in the lexicon.** Jab (vocab) and delayed swing (translate) need an English prompt, and the lexicon has none. M1's hand-written questions carry their own English. M2 adds a `gloss_en` field to lexicon entries (extending the file) and flags it for review.
26. **Content to flag for native review** (not changed now):
    - Mass nouns (`vino`, `leche`, `sopa`) in `t_plural_object` give odd sentences (`las leches`). M2 should add a `mass` tag and exclude those nouns from plural templates.
    - `mirar` + animals (`lobo`, `cuervo`) may want personal *a* (`al lobo`).
    - Feminine nouns with stressed initial *a* (`agua`, `hacha`) take `el` and need an exception tag before any are added.
    - The `r1_chapel` sign "La puerta del norte está cerrada" sits next to an open north exit. Read it as referring to the boss gate further on.
27. **Reading choices need a correct exit.** Signs have no machine-readable answer. M3 adds an optional `reading: { correct: Direction, components, wrongCost }` to room contents. Signs without it are flavour and record nothing.
28. **`$back` after a shortcut.** `$back` is whichever room the player last came from. After entering the cellar through the bonfire shortcut, both `$back` and `north` lead to the bonfire. That is acceptable.

**Mastery**

29. **Blind rounds happen in combat.** CLAUDE.md says combat answers never mark Learned, but SPEC counts "blind rounds where the enemy is hidden" (such as the boss's blind phase) as untelegraphed. Reading: blind-round answers count toward mastery even inside a fight. The exclusion covers ordinary exchanges with a visible enemy and attack icon.

**Milestone 1 scope**

30. **Playwright smoke in M1.** Prompt 1 lists only dev/build/typecheck/lint/test, but CLAUDE.md requires `test:e2e`. It is added in M1. The smoke test loads `/?seed=N`, recomputes the correct directions in Node from the same pure content module and seed, and presses them. No debug hook exposes answers in the page. Dependency: `@playwright/test`, needed for the required smoke test.
31. **Text before the pixel font exists.** M1 draws text with `fillText` on the scaled display canvas (crisp, full accent support), not on the 320×180 buffer. The pixel font choice stays open until M4.
32. **M1 question set.** 20 hand-written questions using only seed-lexicon Spanish: jab, overhead, delayed (no feint yet), and one two-blank combo. No grab in M1, because it needs audio. Directions are assigned when a question is drawn, not stored in the hand-written data.

### 2026-11-17: Milestone 1 build

33. **Blind-bot bound is one-sided.** The test asserts each bot scores at most 25% + 2 points, matching "no better than chance". A bot well *below* chance could be exploited by doing the opposite, so each such text bot also has its complement tested ("anything but the longest card", and so on). *Why:* the longest-card bot scores about 22%, because the combo's fixed answers (levanta, llevo) are short. That is not a leak. Measured rates for all 12 bots fall between 21.9% and 26.8%.
34. **JSON imports use `with { type: 'json' }`.** Playwright's Node loader requires it, and Vite and TypeScript accept it. This lets the smoke test import the same pure content module the game uses.
35. **Smoke-test hook.** The page writes `data-phase`, `data-question` (`id#seed`, the same tag printed on screen for bug reports), `data-strike` (blank index) and `data-deadline` on `<body>`. None of these is the answer. The test recomputes the answer from the seed in Node.
36. **What the riposte asks you to type.** You type each strike's correct card text. For vocabulary that is the article and noun (`el libro`). For translation it is the whole sentence. Punctuation and case are ignored.
37. **State additions.** `EncounterState` adds `idle` (before `start`) and `riposteResult` (shows the verdicts for `RIPOSTE_RESULT_MS`) to SPEC §9's union.
38. **Hidden tab.** Wind-up deadlines run on real time, so if the tab is hidden during a wind-up, the strike times out as a hit. This is acceptable for M1. Pausing on `visibilitychange` belongs with Hollow mode and settings in M4.
39. **Text-shape residue from the fixed combo.** The combo always asks 3sg and 1sg, which tilts the deck slightly toward short, prefix-shaped answers. The "prefix of most other cards" bot measures 26.8%, inside the bound. Generated content in M2 picks persons uniformly, which removes this.

### 2026-11-17: Milestone 2 build (procedural content)

40. **Uniform answers, feature first.** The generator draws each subject's person uniformly and then a pronoun of that person. It draws each noun's gender uniformly, then a verb that can take a noun of that gender (slot rule), then the noun. Distractors are a uniform random subset of the blank's paradigm. Given the four cards on screen, each is equally likely to be the answer. The deck gives every template equal weight, so the singular and plural article templates balance each other. `t_indefinite_plural` was added for the same reason: with only the singular indefinite template, `unos`/`unas` would never be the answer and a bot could rule out half the cards. A generator test checks the answer spread inside each card set over the deck mix.
41. **Combo distractors leave out the other subject's person.** In `Ella ___ la espada y yo ___ el escudo.`, blank 2's cards never include the 3sg form, and blank 1's never include the 1sg form. Otherwise, once blank 1 is revealed (`levanta`), a player could rule out the blank-2 card with the same ending (`lleva`) without knowing any Spanish. The blind-bot suite has an "avoid / match the ending of the last revealed answer" bot pair for this.
42. **The deck draws templates independently, not in shuffled passes.** With passes, the template just used cannot come up again until the pass ends, so the previous answer predicts the next template. The "avoid the last ending" bot scored 33% on singular article questions (26.6% overall). Independent uniform draws bring it to 23.8%. All 14 blind bots now measure 23.8% to 25.7%.
43. **"Prefer realistic learner errors."** Distractors come only from the errors a template varies: wrong person for verbs, wrong gender or number for articles, another word for vocabulary. They are not weighted toward the "most common" error, because weights that depend on the answer would let the card set reveal it (#40). Article blanks always show the whole paradigm, so `el espada` is always a card for `la espada`. The fourth card changes two features (`los` for `la`), per #21. Any candidate whose text is also a correct answer is dropped (tested with a fake verb whose yo and él forms are the same).
44. **Capitalisation.** A pattern ending in `.`, `?` or `!` is a sentence: its first letter is capitalised (skipping `¿ ¡`), so `Tú`, `Él`. A bare phrase (the vocabulary card `la espada`) stays lower case. If a sentence opens with a blank, the four cards are capitalised instead of the following word.
45. **Schema and build check.** zod is a dev dependency, used only at build time and in tests. The game bundle imports schema *types* only (checked: no zod in `dist`). `validate.ts` adds the cross-reference checks: pattern ↔ slots, `requires` → verb slot, `agreesWith` / `distinctFrom` / `distinctPersonFrom` targets, every `tags` / `excludeTags` / verb `takes` tag carried by some noun, every noun slot fillable under the slot rule, blanks and `vary` lists, `tests` and `gloss_en` placeholders, pronouns for all six persons, room exits, doors, and items. A Vite plugin runs it in `buildStart`, so `npm run build` and `npm run dev` fail on bad content. `vite-node` (already in the tree through vitest, now pinned) runs `npm run review-pack`. `@types/node` is referenced only from that script.
46. **Template format extensions** (additions, nothing removed): noun-slot `tags` / `excludeTags`; template `gloss_en` (English prompt pattern); `blanks: ["$sentence"]` for a whole-phrase answer, with `vary: ["lemma"]` (vocabulary) or two features making a 2x2 grid (translation, as in #4); pronoun `gloss_en`; lexicon `gloss_en` (#25). A verb agrees with the nearest subject before it. Test strings now use the canonical ids from #23 (`{verb}:present:{person}` → `v_levantar:present:3sg`, `{noun}:plural` → `n_manzana:plural`).
47. **Mass nouns (#26).** `sopa`, `vino` and `leche` are tagged `mass` and excluded from the plural and indefinite templates. `mirar` + animal (`miro el lobo` vs `al lobo`) is unchanged and listed as a question in the review pack.
48. **Hand-written questions removed.** Jab, delayed swing, overhead and combo questions all come from the eight Zone 1 templates. The bug-report tag is now `templateId#seed`, and `questionFromTag` rebuilds the question from it (the smoke test uses it).
49. **Accepted residue, revisit at playtest.** The blind bots see card text only. A player reading the sentence can pick up shallow cues: a noun ending in `-s` takes a plural article, `-a` usually takes `la`, and some vocabulary words are cognates (`vino`, `sopa`, `torre`). Picking these up is learning the grammar the question tests, so this is not treated as a leak. The litmus playtest should watch for it.
50. **Seed contract.** A question is a pure function of (template, seed, content). The generator draws in a fixed order: subjects, then each noun (with the verb it requires), then any verb left over, then distractors and card placement blank by blank. Changing that order, the templates, or the lexicon changes what an old seed produces, so `id#seed` tags only reproduce a question against the same content version. Bug reports should give the commit.
51. **Slot references point backwards.** `distinctFrom` and `distinctPersonFrom` must name a slot earlier in the pattern, so the generator can fill slots in a single pass. The validator enforces this.
52. **Prompt text.** Verb blank: the infinitive in brackets, `(levantar)`. A lone article blank: `Choose the article.` Combos: one hint per blank, joined with ` … `. Vocabulary and translation: the English gloss in quotes. Prompts never contain a card's text (tested).
53. **Vocabulary details.** The component is the bare noun id (`n_espada`). Distractors are other nouns from the same slot filter, each with its own correct article, never one with the same English gloss.
54. **Review pack layout.** 25 rows per template (200 ÷ 8), scanning seeds from 1 and skipping repeats of the same filled sentence and prompt. The file is generated and committed. Regenerate it after any content change and never edit it by hand.
55. **Handoff file.** `docs/PROGRESS.md` holds per-milestone status and what the next session needs to know. It uses upper case to match `SPEC.md` and `DECISIONS.md`.

### 2026-11-17: Milestone 3 build (learning and world)

56. **`src/world/` is a pure-logic directory.** CLAUDE.md's layout has no home for room-graph rules (exits, `$back`, doors, the gate check, ghosts, echoes). They are pure, so they live in `src/world/` and ESLint treats it like `combat`, `content`, `learning` and `save` (no browser globals).
57. **Scheduler details.** Time is a whole local day number (local midnight, read only in `platform/clock.ts`). A component the player has never answered is not scheduled. On its first answer it starts in box 1, due that day. A correct answer moves it up one box and sets the due day to today + the new box's interval. A wrong answer sends it to box 1, due tomorrow. A component climbs **at most one box per day**: without that, grinding one fight would push a word to box 5 in an afternoon and defeat the spacing. A miss clears the day's promotion, so a ghost beaten the same day can still lift it one box.
58. **Due-first, ghosts and echoes never choose the answer.** Serving the exact missed cell (`levanta`) again would let a blind player win by picking the form that was revealed when they missed. Instead the picker draws from the normal uniform deck stream and keeps the first question that shows the target **on any card**, as answer or distractor. Acceptance depends only on the card set, and M2 made the answer uniform within each card set (#40), so the target is the answer one time in four. For articles this matters: forcing `t_definite_article` alone would make only `el`/`la` possible, and accepting on the card set keeps the singular and plural templates balanced. Measured: the target card is the answer 23.8% (verbs), 25.0% (articles), 25.0% (vocabulary). Memory bots that pick (or avoid) any card they missed before score 25.5% / 25.3% over 10,000 strikes against the due-first picker. `picker.test.ts` runs both bots in CI with the 2-point tolerance from #33. Components carried by the sentence and never shown on a card (`n_manzana:plural`) are not targeted in combat. Bonfire reviews and the plain deck still serve them.
59. **An untelegraphed miss resets mastery.** "Two correct answers on different days" with no reset can be reached by guessing four-way reading choices over enough days. An untelegraphed miss clears the streak, and it also un-learns a Learned component. Combat misses touch the schedule only (rule 6 both ways). Expected guesses for a 1-in-4 guesser to reach Learned: 20 tries.
60. **Bonfire review is typed recall.** The SPEC does not fix the format. A typed fill-in with no cards, no enemy and no timer is the strongest untelegraphed test and gives a guesser nothing to work with. It uses single-blank templates only (no combo, no whole-sentence translation). The order is due components not yet reviewed today, then met-but-unlearned ones (lowest box first), then components never met. `REVIEW_ROUND_SIZE` = 5 (default, not in design doc). A missing accent is forgiven in Zone 1 and counts as correct (#11).
61. **Flasks.** Press F during a wind-up. Two wrong cards, chosen by a separate flask RNG stream, are greyed out (struck through and dimmed) and cannot be picked. One flask per strike. A correct answer with a flask does not promote the component (`assisted`), but a miss still demotes it. Using a flask reveals information on purpose and costs a limited resource; the blind bots never drink one.
62. **Reading choices (completes #27).** Room contents gain `reading: { goal_en, correct, components, wrongCost }`, validated (the sign must exist, the answer must be an exit, at least three exits). Leaving the room records an untelegraphed answer. Only the bonfire sign has one ("Find the well." → west). No other seed sign offers a directional choice, and adding one would mean writing new Spanish. Each reading counts **once per save**, because after one walk the player knows the map and the choice no longer tests reading. Its components (`w_izquierda`, `w_pozo`) are not in the gate's universe, since no template teaches them. Exits never name the room behind them, even for visited rooms.
63. **Ghosts.** For each missed strike, the first card-representable component it tested becomes a ghost in the room where it was missed. Each room keeps the first three, with no duplicates. A ghost is a short fight: `GHOST_STANCE_MAX` = 1 (any parry breaks stance) and `GHOST_HP` = 15 (one typed riposte word). Its questions target its component by card membership (#58). Ghosts do not block exits; the room's regular enemy blocks everything except the way back. SPEC says beating a ghost "moves its component up a box". Here it moves the components actually answered, which is the ghost's own component one time in four. There is no bonus promotion without evidence.
64. **Death and echoes.** XP comes from wins (`ENEMY_XP` 50, `GHOST_XP` 10, defaults). Nothing spends XP yet, so the echo holds all of it. Dying drops an echo in the room, held by the foe that killed you (the regular enemy, or that ghost). Dying again before recovering it loses the old one. You wake at the last bonfire with vitality and flasks refilled, regular enemies respawn (Souls convention), and ghosts stay. The echo fight targets the component of the last strike you missed ("the same target word"), by card membership.
65. **Saving.** Bonfires save, as SPEC says. Learning progress also autosaves on every room change, fight end and review answer, because losing reviews to a closed tab is worse for a learning game. Loading always puts you at the last bonfire. The save is validated field by field with hand-written checks, since zod stays out of the bundle (#45). A corrupt save is reported on the title screen and is not overwritten until you start a new game. Export downloads a JSON file and import opens a file picker. Both are on the map screen and the title.
66. **The boss arena is a placeholder.** The boss fight is a later prompt. The arena shows a note, and ↓ walks back out.
67. **Room attack types do not restrict the deck yet.** Restricting each enemy to its listed attack would break the template balance #40 relies on, and `grab` (listening) does not exist yet. Every regular enemy uses the whole Zone 1 deck; the JSON attack names the enemy. Items and the NPC are not interactive yet.
68. **The gate counts a fixed universe.** `zoneComponents(1)` enumerates every component the Zone 1 templates can test: 120 (66 verb cells, 8 article cells, 19 plural nouns, 27 vocabulary nouns). A test checks it against 3,000 seeds per template. At 80% the gate needs 96 Learned.
69. **Dev keys.** In `npm run dev` builds only: F8 skips a day forward and F9 marks every Zone 1 component Learned. They exist to test spacing and the gate by hand. Production builds pass `dev: false` and the keys do nothing.
70. **What combat records.** Each resolved strike updates the schedule for its components (`strikeComponents`: all of them on a single-blank question, the blank's own on a combo). Riposte typing does not update the schedule.
71. **World fights publish the same test tags as the practice fight** (`data-phase`, `data-question`, `data-strike`, `data-deadline`, plus `data-scene` and `data-room`), under #35's rule: never the answer. The practice fight stays at `/?seed=N` for the smoke test, and `/` is now the world.
72. **`$back` follows each room's own way back (fixes #28 as first built).** M3 first made `$back` mean "the room you just left". Going lane → chapel → gate and then back to the chapel made the chapel's `$back` point at the gate, so ↑ and ↓ both led north and there was no way home. Now each room remembers the room it was last entered from going forward (`GameState.backOf`). Walking a `$back` exit leaves the destination's entry alone. #28 still holds: the cellar's way back is whichever side you entered it from. Older saves without `backOf` load with an empty one.

### 2026-11-17: Seals and meta-progression (designer change)

73. **The boss gate needs seals, not 80% Learned.** The designer wants a zone clearable in one sitting; Learned needs answers on two different days, so a Learned gate could not be. Each zone declares seals in its rooms JSON, one per grammar aspect. Each seal is won from one warden, an enemy whose deck is only that seal's templates: Zone 1's are Articles (cellar), Verbs (chapel) and Words (orchard). Every Zone 1 template belongs to exactly one seal. Wardens have `WARDEN_HP` 90 and give `WARDEN_XP` 150 (defaults). They never respawn, and seals last through rests and deaths until New Game. The gate opens with every seal. This is safe under rule zero because wardens are fights. The blind-bot suite now runs every bot on every seal deck as well as the whole deck. Rates are 23.8%–25.9%, and no guesser wins a fight. The articles warden runs 1,500 fights: its questions are all high-damage overheads, so a guesser dies in about four strikes. It is a partial exception to #67: wardens use a restricted deck because each seal group is balanced on its own (articles keeps singular and plural templates per definiteness, as #40 needs). Regular enemies keep the whole deck. A due, ghost or echo target the seal deck cannot show is not targeted in a warden fight. **Boss plan:** phase 1 uses the articles deck, phase 2 the present deck, and phase 3 (blind) mixes all three seal decks.
74. **Flask power starts at 1, maxes at 2.** This is the designer's change to the design doc's "greys out two". A flask greys one wrong card, and the flask perk raises it to two. It never greys all three: two cards always stay, so the player still has to read. Encounter clamps any configured power to `FLASK_POWER_MAX`.
75. **Learned is meta-progression.** Knowledge moved out of the run into a profile (`lingua-dark/profile`) that New Game keeps. The run save keeps seals, wardens beaten and the rest. Save format v2: version 1 saves migrate on load, their learner becoming the profile (only if no profile exists yet). An export file bundles run and profile; importing a file without a profile keeps the current one. Perks come from `PERKS` in `tuning.ts`:
    - 10 Learned: +10 vitality
    - 25: flask power 2
    - 40: +3 riposte damage
    - 60: +10 vitality
    - 80: +1 flask per rest (the only flask-count perk; flask rewards stay sparse)
    - 100: +10 vitality
    - 115: +3 riposte damage

    Perks read `bestLearned`, the highest Learned count ever reached, so un-learning a word (#59) does not shrink max vitality mid-run. Learned still needs untelegraphed typed answers on two days, so a player who cannot read Spanish never earns a perk.
76. **Dev keys.** F10 grants every seal (dev builds only), next to F8 (skip a day) and F9 (learn all, which now fills the profile and so unlocks every perk).

### 2026-11-17: Milestone 4 build (art, audio, settings, boss)

77. **The boss's "longer combos" are chained questions (flurries).** SPEC 3.7 asks for longer combos in each boss phase, but the only combo template has two blanks and the articles deck has no combo at all. Writing 3- or 4-blank templates would mean new Spanish. Instead an Encounter takes `chain`: one exchange serves that many questions back to back, each with its own wind-up and window. The combo-clear bonus needs every strike of the whole chain parried. A stance break skips the rest of the chain and ripostes the question that broke it (as #9). `BOSS_CHAIN` is [2, 2, 3] (default). Each phase is its own Encounter with its own HP (`BOSS_PHASE_HP` 60) and stance meter (`BOSS_STANCE_MAX` 100). Vitality carries from phase to phase, flasks are shared, and a title card opens each phase. The boss data gains `name_en` ("The Hollow Bell", English UI) and `phaseDecks`, mapping each phase to seals (validated): articles, present, then all three for the blind phase, as #73 planned. The boss never respawns (`bossesBeaten` in the save; older saves load with an empty list), holds the echo of a player it kills, and gives `BOSS_XP` 500. Questions come from a chained deck of independent draws, so #40/#42 still hold; the blind-bot suite runs every bot on each phase (23.8%–25.9%, no wins).
78. **Pixel font: an original bitmap font in code ("Hollow 5"), not a third-party TTF.** It guarantees the coverage the spec asks about. It needs no download and no licence, it is original art (CLAUDE.md), and it draws on the same pixel grid as the sprites. Glyphs sit in an 11-row box: two rows for capital accents, 7-row capitals, a 5-row x-height, and two descender rows. Lower-case accents have a clear row above the letter. `font.test.ts` proves á é í ó ú ñ ü ¿ ¡ and the capitals: each accented glyph is its base letter with a mark above, and ¿ ¡ hang below the baseline. It also checks full printable ASCII and every character in the game's string literals and content JSON, so a new UI symbol without a glyph fails the tests. Unknown characters draw as a hollow box, never silently. `/font-test.html` renders the set at every size the game uses.
79. **"Prompt at 2x scale" reading.** Body text is drawn at one font pixel per half art pixel (size 6, "1x"). The prompt is drawn at one font pixel per art pixel (size 12, "2x"), the same grid as the sprites, on a solid dark band for contrast. Text is drawn on the display canvas in whole screen pixels. `maxWidth` shrinks a line one screen pixel at a time and never blurs it. Larger text multiplies every size by `LARGE_TEXT_SCALE` (1.5); lines that would overflow step back down.
80. **Speech: es-ES voice only, never a blank before its reveal.** With no es-ES voice, or no speechSynthesis, the game is text-only. Another Spanish voice is not used, because the dialect is Castilian (see Dialect, at the top). What is spoken:
    - the completed sentence after the last strike of a question that did not break stance;
    - the completed sentence after the riposte verdict (a break leads to the riposte, which asks for those words, so nothing is spoken before it);
    - the bonfire review sentence after the answer is shown;
    - a room's sign on arrival;
    - in a blind round, the sentence with each blank read as a pause, when its wind-up opens. These are the words already on screen, the same for every answer direction.

    R repeats the last line. Everything spoken is on screen, and a ♪ marks the prompt while speech plays.
81. **Sound cues come from the view, and every one has a caption.** `cuesBetween(prev, next)` reads only the phase, the Animation and the riposte verdicts, so sound cannot carry more than the animation. The wind-up cue is the same for every question and direction (tested, and folded into the blind-bot wind-up identity test). `CUE_VISUAL` names the visual twin of every cue. Most also get a caption ("♪ clang") that shows even with sound off. The wind-up and menu cues have no caption, because the wind-up animation and the menu cursor show them. The Animation gains `resolved` (parry, perfect or hit; null before lock-in), so cues and the reduced-motion parry mark do not depend on catching the 50 ms flash.
82. **Hollow mode (no timers).** `Encounter.untimed`: a wind-up never times out and the deadline bar is hidden. An answer after the normal window parries at quality 0, so the drain sits at `STANCE_FLOOR`; earlier answers still drain more. It can be switched mid-fight. Provisional answer to the open question: Hollow changes nothing about mastery. Combat never counts; review, reading and blind answers count the same with or without timers. The review is already untimed, and timers test speed, not knowledge. Playtest logs (M5) record Hollow per exchange, so the designer can revisit this.
83. **Game time pauses (fixes #38).** `platform/gameTime.ts` is performance.now() minus paused time. The loop, input timestamps and encounters all read it. It pauses while the tab is hidden and while the settings menu is open, so neither times out a wind-up.
84. **Reduced motion.** No full-screen parry or hit flash. Instead the parry ring, the clean-parry star and the hit cross stay on screen through the resolve. No hit shake, no room fade (rooms cut), no drifting far layer and no bonfire flicker. The wind-up animation and the hit-stop stay, since they carry the game. The default follows `prefers-reduced-motion`.
85. **Sprites.** Atlas tags are `<character>/<animation>` with the SPEC 8 frame counts (`requiredTags` in `combat/sprites.ts`). Frames are anchored at the bottom centre. Directional sets exist for north, east and south; west draws east mirrored. `enemyFrame`/`playerFrame` pick frames from the Animation alone. During a wind-up the frame is `floor(progress × 6)`, one shared animation time-stretched to the window, and the player holds one guard frame. Idle cycles use the clock only outside wind-ups. Real art loads from `/art/atlas.json` (Aseprite JSON, hash or array) plus its PNG. Any character or tag it lacks falls back to the generated placeholder sheet, which uses the same format, so art can arrive one character at a time.
86. **The blind phase.** It hides the enemy (fog), the attack icon and the strike animation. It keeps the prompt, the cards, the deadline bar (unless Hollow), the stance and HP bars, and the ✓/✗ card marks after each strike. The player's parry and hit poses still play after lock-in. Answers are recorded as `blind`, untelegraphed per #29, unless a flask greyed cards on that strike; then they count as `combat`.
87. **Wardens look like wardens.** Warden sprites wear a three-spiked crown and a seal diamond on the chest, and the room label gets a ◆. These are shapes, not colours. A test checks that every warden in the room data has a warden sprite and every boss a boss sprite.
88. **Settings** live under their own key (`lingua-dark/settings`): reduced motion, larger text, Hollow, sound effects, music, speech. Bad or missing fields fall back to defaults. O opens the menu from any scene except while typing. The menu routes input one event at a time, so a key pressed right after closing reaches the game.
89. **Not built in M4** (Prompt 4 does not ask for them):
    - Grab (listening) attacks. Before building one, the design needs a call: matching heard Spanish to written cards can be done by sound alone without understanding, so a grab built that way might be winnable without Spanish.
    - Feints (#2).
    - Sweep (word order).
    - NPC dialogue and items.
90. **Palette.** 32 colours in `platform/palette.ts`: desaturated blues and greys, warm greys and browns for the bonfire and the boss, bonfire orange as the warm accent, and pale yellow as the signal colour. Every state that a colour marks also has a shape or word.
